One request, five stops

  1. Import

    You, or a scan of the inbox

    Each message is read before anything is saved. You see who sent it, whether the mailbox is one person or a shared office address, and whether it joins a lead you already have.

  2. Sort

    Triage agent

    A real request becomes a lead with a checklist: rooms, size, timing, address. A newsletter is left alone. A message that tries to instruct the agent is quarantined, with no draft.

  3. Reply

    Reply agent, then you

    The draft asks only what the checklist is still missing. It sits in the conversation until you send it, edit it, or take the thread over and write it yourself.

  4. Estimate

    You, from your rate book

    The first estimate turns the lead into a job. Suggested lines use rates you already approved. A line with no rate has no price. Approving records that exact version.

  5. Invoice

    You, then Stripe

    After you record that the customer accepted, you can invoice a deposit and the rest. The customer pays on Stripe's page. A refund stays a task for you.

The aim is a business day: a real inquiry gets a next step, a no with a reason, or a handoff to you. That clock is the product's target, not a number the app measures yet.

Two agents. The rest is rules.

Triage

Reads one message and says how sure it is that this is work you offer. It pulls out only facts the sender wrote, and quotes the words it used. A size it is unsure of stays flagged on the lead, so you can see the soft spot.

Reply

Writes a short note in the voice of the shop. It cannot name a price, a discount, a start date, or a time you can visit, and it does not say it is an AI. A draft that slips and makes a promise is thrown out. A plain template is offered instead.

Who the customer is, which lead an email joins, the estimate math, and the Stripe invoice are ordinary code, not a model. If Claude is unavailable, a rule-based reader still sorts the mail and the screen tells you which one answered.

Five places, one thumb

  • Needs you

    The home screen. Drafts to send, customers to confirm, estimates over a helper's limit, invoices that need a retry. Most urgent first.

  • Inbox

    The conversation stays with the lead. A draft is visibly unsent, with a note of what the agent used and what it refused to say. You send, edit, reject, or take over.

  • Import

    Scan the connected inbox, or type a field note for something you heard on site when there is no email yet. Pasting a message is there for trying the flow.

  • Jobs

    Rooms, lines, and a total you can stand behind. Change the lines after you approve and that approval no longer applies, so an old price cannot go out by accident.

  • Invoices

    Deposit and remainder, each tied to one estimate so a retry cannot create a second bill. Stripe emails the customer on file. Refresh payment reads what Stripe already knows.

What never leaves without you

A customer-facing word

Sorting a message and drafting a reply can happen before you look. Sending it cannot. A cover note may quote the approved total and no other amount, and only while the lines still match that approval.

A price

You approve an exact version: the lines, the total, and the time. A helper can draft an estimate. If you allow them to approve, they still stop at the limit you set. Over it, the estimate waits on your queue.

Who the customer is

A personal mailbox can join its open lead. A shared address such as office@ cannot, until you say it is one person. A possible duplicate offers merge or keep separate. No agent makes that call.

Money

You record that the customer accepted, and you decide to invoice. The customer pays on Stripe's page. ContractorFlow does not store a card or move the money, and nothing in the app can issue a refund.

An office assistant is not a second login yet. You can preview the day as they would see it: drafts and estimate drafts, not invoices, not the rules, and not the customer's yes.

Ridgeline Floors is the fictional shop in the working prototype, and Dana is its owner. The people, addresses, and prices on this page are that demo. The walkthrough describes the product as it runs: not a ledger, a calendar, or a customer portal.
